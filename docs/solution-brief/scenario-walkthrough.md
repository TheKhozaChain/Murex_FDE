# Scenario Walkthrough

> Start with the [visual solution overview](../solution-overview.md). This document is the canonical detailed scenario reference.

## How to read the scenarios

Murex FDE Workbench contains three executable synthetic scenarios. They are deliberately different because a useful support workflow must distinguish among:

1. a genuine operational fault that supports a controlled recovery;
2. a material but legitimate business movement where remediation would be wrong; and
3. a critical case where the first evidence packet is not sufficient to confirm a cause.

All three use the same workflow and component boundaries. The scenario registry selects typed input data and deterministic tool adapters; it does not select a predetermined final answer. The recommendation is constructed from executed facts and retrieved guidance, then subjected to citation and policy checks.

The figures, system names, incidents, actors, and actions below are fictional. Recovery actions are simulations and do not contact production systems.

## Flagship walkthrough: `HVB-2847`

The detailed sequence is shown in [Technical stale market-data sequence](../diagrams/technical/stale-market-data-sequence.md).

### 1. Incident intake

The fictional Daily Market Risk report shows an 18.4% increase in APAC FX delta. The associated batch `RISK_APAC_010` and its dependencies report `SUCCEEDED`.

That successful status is useful context, but it is not treated as proof that every input was current. The scenario is designed to expose a common support error: confusing successful processing with valid source data.

### 2. Deterministic evidence collection

The shared dispatcher runs scenario-specific deterministic tools. For `HVB-2847`, they:

- validate the server-owned payload;
- compare the USD/JPY observation timestamp with the required freshness boundary;
- calculate exposure for positions sensitive to the affected currency pair;
- inspect movement concentration and population reconciliation;
- confirm batch and dependency status;
- apply the configured materiality threshold; and
- check that the required evidence types are present.

The resulting facts include:

- the USD/JPY observation is stale;
- AUD 12.8m of synthetic exposure is affected;
- 93% of the movement is concentrated in the affected population;
- population control passed; and
- the batch and dependencies succeeded.

These values are calculated from checked-in structured inputs. They are not generated by the synthesis provider.

### 3. Attributable guidance retrieval

The local retriever ranks a closed corpus of fictional runbooks and policies. Relevant approved guidance says to verify the timestamp and exposure, contact Market Data Operations, hold report distribution, and avoid direct rate modification.

Each retrieval result retains its document identifier, version, approval status, trust category, relevance score, matched terms, and excerpt. A deliberately malicious unapproved note is present in the corpus, but instruction-like content receives a strong penalty and cannot qualify as approved citation evidence.

### 4. Structured synthesis

The default provider is `DeterministicMockSynthesiser`. It demonstrates the provider contract without making a network call or requiring model credentials.

It creates a versioned recommendation with:

- an outcome classification of `stale_market_data`;
- observed facts and candidate causes;
- a cited diagnosis;
- confidence and a confidence rationale;
- remaining uncertainty;
- a recommended action;
- risk and blast radius;
- preconditions, validation, and rollback; and
- analyst and stakeholder summaries.

The recommendation is not permission to act. Its initial operational effect is explicitly `none`.

### 5. Validation and policy

The validator parses the recommendation against a runtime schema and checks all cited identifiers. It also rejects contradictions such as claiming the batch failed when deterministic evidence says it succeeded, or proposing direct modification of a protected production-like record.

The policy engine checks citation validity, structured output, required evidence, protected-record mutation, confidence, severity, and action constraints. A passing result is `approval_required`, not automatic execution.

### 6. Human decision

The interface presents the reviewer with the recommendation version, cited evidence snapshot, action scope, risk, blast radius, preconditions, validation plan, rollback, and policy result.

The current implementation uses the clearly labelled fictional identity `demo.support.analyst`. The reviewer may approve or reject. The approval records the exact recommendation version and evidence identifiers. If the recommendation is superseded, the earlier approval cannot authorise execution.

### 7. Action gate and synthetic remediation

The only permitted action for this incident is:

`refresh_fx_market_data_and_rerun_risk_controls`

Before execution, the workflow rechecks:

- current citation and fact validation;
- policy permission;
- human approval and approval scope;
- recommendation version;
- evidence completeness;
- confidence threshold;
- incident outcome;
- action allow-list;
- source confirmation;
- report-distribution hold; and
- idempotency.

If any check fails, the action is rejected and an audit event is appended.

The successful path simulates verifying Market Data Operations confirmation, publishing an approved current USD/JPY observation to isolated synthetic staging, running only the affected APAC controls, and gathering a new evidence packet. It cannot run arbitrary SQL, shell commands, generated code, external APIs, or production writes.

### 8. Independent validation and closure

The workflow creates five fresh validation records:

1. USD/JPY freshness;
2. scoped APAC risk rerun;
3. exposure population;
4. risk reconciliation; and
5. report-distribution state.

A deterministic resolution policy examines those records. If all pass, the incident becomes `resolved` and records `RESOLVED`. If freshness or reconciliation is forced to fail in the negative test, distribution remains held, the run becomes `requires_escalation`, and the outcome is `VALIDATION_FAILED`.

Execution success is therefore not treated as business success. The synthesis provider cannot override the result.

## Scenario two: `HVB-2829`

### The question

The fictional crude derivatives desk reports an AUD 6.1m day-on-day P&L movement above its review threshold. A simplistic incident system might assume that a large movement requires technical remediation.

### What the tools establish

Deterministic calculations decompose the movement into market-price movement, position sensitivity, carry, and new trades. The residual is within the configured AUD 25,000 tolerance. Trade population, valuation timestamp, currency conversion, batch, and dependencies all pass.

### The safe outcome

The structured recommendation classifies the movement as `legitimate_business_movement`. It asks Product Control to review and publish an evidence-cited explanation. It explicitly says not to repair data or rerun the batch.

Scenario-specific validation rejects a provider response that invents a technical defect, recommends repair, proposes an unnecessary batch rerun, or escalates to technical support without evidence. This scenario demonstrates that safe AI assistance must know when not to act.

## Scenario three: `HVB-2822`

### First pass: insufficient current evidence

The fictional Liquidity Coverage Pack has received 13 of 14 source segments. The source reader timed out, the source manifest is unavailable, current mapping validation is inconclusive, and a similar historical incident involved a mapping defect.

The workflow separates those signals:

- the timeout is a current observation, not proof of cause;
- the historical mapping incident is context, not proof of the current cause;
- the missing manifest prevents population completeness from being established;
- the mapping result is inconclusive; and
- the internal sign-off window is at risk.

The recommendation records `unconfirmed_critical_cause` at 41% confidence. Policy returns `fail_closed`. The permitted human decision is limited to an `escalation_disposition`; the reviewer cannot approve a confirmed resolution or recovery.

### Evidence expansion

The reviewer may request more investigation. A separate audited transition gathers synthetic supplemental evidence:

- the authoritative manifest requires 14 segments;
- the transfer ledger shows `LIQ_POS_14.csv` was not delivered;
- current mapping passes;
- there were no relevant configuration changes; and
- the partial Datamart row count is outside recent volumes.

The provider produces recommendation version 2 and ranks upstream non-delivery as the probable cause. Current evidence rules out the historical mapping hypothesis and treats the timeout as a downstream symptom rather than the cause.

### Scoped recovery

After policy reassessment and human approval, the only permitted action is:

`reingest_liquidity_segment_and_resume_datamart`

The simulator re-ingests only the missing synthetic segment, resumes from the documented source-reader recovery point, and validates source segments, Datamart population, mapping, report rendering, and the SLA alert. As with the FX case, deterministic validation owns the final state.

## Evaluation coverage

The three golden cases execute through the application workflow, and negative tests cover unsupported claims, unsafe decisions and failed recovery paths. The canonical measures, limitations and commands are documented in [Evaluation methodology](../evaluation-methodology.md).
